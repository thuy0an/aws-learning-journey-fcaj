# Event Report: FCAJ — Agentic AI Build Week

### Event Overview

**FCAJ — Agentic AI Build Week** was the closing and experience-sharing event for the Agentic AI Build Week hackathon. Engineers and technology students worked in teams under 24–48 hour time pressure to build AI Agent products for real business problems, then presented their demos to the judging panel.

### Event Objectives

- Encourage learners to move beyond theory through hands-on work.
- Develop teamwork, product thinking, knowledge sharing, and basic investor-pitching skills.
- Show how AI and cloud technology can improve the way teams work and innovate.

### Speakers and Teams

- **Nguyen Gia Hung** — Head of Solution Architects, AWS Vietnam.
- **Joseph Barazota** — Head of Technology at Asian.
- **Outstanding teams:** One Team, Signal Scout, Plan, 3K (TRK), and Six Pillars.

### Key Highlights

#### AI Agent solutions

- **One Team — First Prize:** An AI chatbot for KFC orders on Zalo and WhatsApp. It uses AWS AgentCore to retain conversation context and reduce incorrect orders caused by hallucinations.
- **Signal Scout — Second Prize:** A multi-agent system that collects and analyzes financial and competitor data to support strategic recommendations and ROI forecasting.
- **Plan:** An AI-native solution that creates Draw.io architecture diagrams, cost estimates, and Terraform infrastructure code from natural-language requirements.
- **3K (Shepherd):** A YOLO, computer-vision, and GenAI solution for real-time crowd monitoring in airports or supermarkets.
- **Six Pillars:** A multi-agent anti-money-laundering workflow that supports KYC, cash-flow, and legal checks to shorten fraud-alert investigation time.

#### Product and architecture mindset

- Find the **pain point** before choosing technology.
- Keep the MVP scope focused; too many features can prevent completion during a hackathon.
- A working demo is the best proof that an idea is practical.
- Complex work should be split among specialized agents and coordinated by a main agent.
- Cost optimization and **human-in-the-loop** approval are important for sensitive systems.

### What I Learned

- Start with users and business problems, then balance features, time, and quality.
- Use multi-agent architecture to control complex workflows and reduce context overload.
- Consider cloud operating costs and use human approval for high-risk decisions.

### Applying the Learning

- Define the target customer, problem, and expected operating cost before implementation.
- Use AI for document collection, review, and alerts, with guardrails to verify outputs.
- Divide AI workflows into manageable tasks and retain human approval for important results.

### Event Experience

The speakers showed how faster experimentation and release cycles can create an advantage in the AI era. The hackathon also gave a realistic view of team pressure, disagreements, AI token limits, network issues during demos, and the need to protect files such as `.env`.

#### Key Takeaways

- **Done beats perfect**: a usable product is more valuable than an unfinished perfect idea.
- Working under pressure with a team and fixing real issues are valuable learning experiences.
- Continuous learning is necessary because technology changes quickly.
